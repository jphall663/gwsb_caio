GAI & Teaching & Learning in Higher Ed 

What INSTRUCTORS should know/do What STUDENTS should know/DO 

●​ AI is ubiquitous - students are using it, and are 
likely to use it in your course. 

●​ Just because you can use GAI for an 
assignment, or other coursework doesn’t mean 
you should, 

●​ There is no way to reliably detect its use. ●​ Your use of GAI may raise an instructor’s 
suspicions. 

●​ Create and communicate a clear, explicit 
acceptable use policy.  

●​ Provide a rationale for the policy.   
●​ Stress student learning. 
●​ Build a community of learners. 

●​ Understand your instructor’s AI use policy.  
●​ Ask questions about the policy if you are 

unsure.  
●​ (MIT does not have one, single AI-use policy - 

don’t assume that the policy in all of your 
subjects will be the same). 

●​ Communicate and discuss your GAI policy 
with your colleagues. 

●​ Consider creating a baseline, department or 
program policy. 

 

●​ Critically examine your learning goals and 
assessments of student learning.  
○​ Are your goals for learning still relevant 
○​ Are your assessments AI-Aware? 
○​ Might the use of AI allow you to achieve 

higher-order, more advanced goals? 

●​ Take time to read and understand the learning 
goals for the course.   

 

https://docs.google.com/document/d/12a5dPvvifWP4itpSkGjVIV1UZgfJDWM7bLGcnLB6gH8/edit?usp=sharing


 

 

 

●​ Convey to students why you have chosen the 
particular learning goals for the course. 

●​ Why is it important that students achieve 
these goals? 

●​ Using AI to do the work only shortcuts your 
learning and may prevent you from achieving 
the course goals. 

●​ Communicate the value and importance of 
your assignments and assessments.  
○​ Let students know why you have chosen 

each of the assignments.  
○​ Be explicit about what students should 

learn by engaging with each assignment 
(and how it measures their achievement of 
particular learning outcomes. 

○​ If applicable, tell students why you are 
asking them not to use GAI (or why you 
are asking them to use GAI in a particular 
way.  

○​ Let them know what they will lose if they 
use AI for their work. 

●​ Just because AI can complete an assignment 
doesn’t mean you shouldn't do it yourself. 

●​ What are you learning or not learning through 
your use of GAI? Be honest with yourself. 

 

 ●​ Critically evaluate all AI output.   
●​ Be a savvy user - don’t be fooled by AI’s rapid 

responses and built-in certainty.   
●​ How do you know the output is accurate, 

complete, and acceptable?

Redesigning your Assessments & Classroom Practices 
 
RE your outcomes for learning (goals): 
If A - you are all set! 
If B: You have 3 choices: 

1.​ Change your course goals so that they are in the 
“A” category and change your assignments to align 
with your new goals 

2.​ Keep your goals but change your assignments  
3.​ Keep your goals AND your assignments

Articulating your AI-use Policy 
 
Articulate your policy, your rationale and expectations/consequences.  Consider the state of AI 
detection software. 

Tips for an Effective AI Syllabus Policy 

1.​ If AI is prohibited or required, explain why. 
2.​ Connect AI use to something familiar (e.g., getting help from a friend or tutor). 
3.​ Provide examples of acceptable and/or unacceptable use. 
4.​ Acknowledge ethical issues such as data privacy, bias, inaccuracy, intellectual property 

violations, environmental impact, etc. 
5.​ Note your AI documentation and citation requirements. This might include screenshots, 

transcripts, documents with “track changes” enabled, and/or relevant citation or 
disclosure guidelines: 

○​ APA AI citations guidelines  
○​ MLA AI citation guidelines 
○​ AI Disclosure Framework 

6.​ Explain how misuse will be addressed. 
7.​ Encourage students to ask questions if your policy is unclear. 

After, Daniel Stanford:
